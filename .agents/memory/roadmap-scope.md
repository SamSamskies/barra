---
name: Roadmap scope
description: Product constraints behind the gradual bar muscle-up curriculum
---

The core path is for a first controlled bar muscle-up, not mastery of rings, added weights, archer pulling, or post-goal advanced skills. Repetition and recovery must remain valid choices without forcing advancement or promising a timeline.

**Why:** The user reported multi-week plateaus under the earlier compressed curriculum and explicitly replaced the earlier requirement to retain its seven milestones and historical progress.

**How to apply:** Future curriculum changes should teach new prerequisites explicitly, lower volume when difficulty changes, and avoid simultaneous increases. Keep assessed placement, real sessions and earned mastery conceptually distinct.

The user prefers mixed sessions with a main movement and small supporting exercises, rather than mostly isolated targets that assume strength will improve without guidance.

**Why:** The user said the simplified update expected more pull-ups without guiding the user to build them, and approved returning to mixed sessions with one progression focus at a time.

**How to apply:** Keep supporting targets stable when the main exercise advances, provide actionable assistance/repeat guidance for plateaus, and guide continued pulling development alongside pushing skills rather than relying on outside training.

Higher-pull skill practice should remain part of a mixed strength session, not replace all regular pulling.

**Why:** The user found a session of one chest-to-bar rep and two dip singles too short and approved retaining reduced strict pull-up and dip work throughout this phase.

**How to apply:** Keep the height skill first while fresh and progress only that skill. Preserve a fixed reduced strength base rather than stacking the entire preceding strength workout on top.

Do not show roadmap-rebuild announcements in onboarding.

**Why:** The user said only they currently use the app and do not need this notice.

**How to apply:** Keep onboarding focused on training and placement, not announcements about past curriculum changes.

Keep the first onboarding page concise and scannable.

**Why:** The user noted that many people do not want to read a lot before starting.

**How to apply:** Use a short introduction and clear start action; keep detailed training and safety guidance in relevant workout previews.

Workout coaching should be concise without simplifying the training program.

**Why:** The user requested less reading and scrolling across previews, active sets, rest, readiness and results, while retaining useful coaching and unchanged progression.

**How to apply:** Prioritize the current target and next action. Put full form, demos, plateau explanations and general recovery guidance behind optional details, but keep essential setup and movement-specific safety visible. Preserve the distinction between practice, controlled targets and actual muscle-up success.

Keep the active-set target number in a circle, use singular “rep” for one-rep targets, and use down/up caret icons for optional-info dropdowns rather than plus/minus buttons.

**Why:** The user explicitly preferred the circle and requested these wording and dropdown changes.

**How to apply:** Preserve these preferences when revising workout screens.
